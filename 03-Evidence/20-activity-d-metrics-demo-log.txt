## Part 1 – Worked example (hand-checkable)

Toy index: `A:{}  B:{e1}  C:{e1,x}  D:{e2}  E:{y}  F:{e3}` (C repeats e1, like an overlap duplicate).
Query requires two facts: group 1 = e1, group 2 = e2. Relevant chunks in the index: B, C (e1), D (e2) → 3.
Ranked result: `B, A, E, D`.

| K | Top-K | Relevant in top-K | Precision@K | Recall@K (chunks) | Hit@K | RR@K | Fact groups satisfied | Coverage@K |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | B | B | 1/1 = 1.000 | 1/3 = 0.333 | 1 | 1.000 | 1/2 | 0.500 |
| 3 | B, A, E | B | 1/3 = 0.333 | 1/3 = 0.333 | 1 | 1.000 | 1/2 | 0.500 |
| 5 | B, A, E, D | B, D | 2/5 = 0.400 | 2/3 = 0.667 | 1 | 1.000 | 2/2 | 1.000 |

Reading the table: at K=3 the list has found e1 (in B) but not e2 → coverage 1/2. At K=5 D is included → coverage 2/2, but precision falls (more non-relevant chunks per slot). Retrieving C as well would raise chunk recall but **not** coverage: e1 is one fact.

**K=3 vs K=5.** Going from 3 to 5 retrieved chunks can only keep or raise Hit, MRR, recall and coverage (more chances to include a relevant chunk) and usually lowers precision (the extra slots are mostly non-relevant). On these 5 dev queries every system reaches full coverage at K=5. The trade-off for the assistant is context size: v1 sends 4 chunks to the LLM, and more chunks mean more prompt tokens (Phase 8). These are development numbers only; the held-out comparison is Phase 7.
